# Format spec

Every episode follows the same five-phase arc. The arc exists to do two things: keep retention up
through a comedic, high-stakes opening, and make the switch to the academic breakdown feel earned
instead of abrupt.

Beat IDs below (B1 to B5) are the ones used in `episodes/_template/script/script.md`.

## The viewer contract

These are promises the series makes in every episode. Breaking one costs more trust than any single
video earns, so they are rules, not preferences.

1. **The clock is real and visible.** The study window is shown on screen as a timer driven by the
   recording timestamps, not typed in afterward.
2. **The pass line is stated on camera before the test starts.** It is never moved after the score is
   known.
3. **The exam is frozen before studying begins.** The host does not see the questions or the key in
   advance. The frozen file is committed (with its hash) before the study window opens.
4. **The baseline is stated candidly.** Zero background beyond basic lived experience, said plainly in
   B1. If that is not true for a domain, pick a different domain.
5. **The score comes from the scorer, not the host.** Show the portal result or the scoring script
   output as it appeared. Do not retype it.
6. **The debrief is labeled as a first-timer's synthesis.** It is not professional advice. For health,
   legal, or safety-adjacent domains (nursing, surveying, and similar) say this on screen in B4.
7. **AI-generated exams are labeled as such on screen.** Real exams are labeled with their source.
8. **Sponsor segments are disclosed.** Use YouTube's paid promotion setting and say it out loud in B5.
9. **The payoff is real.** Reward and punishment happen as promised. Nothing about the outcome is
   staged to match a script.

## B1. Hook and constraints (0:00 to 0:45)

- State the target exam or domain in the first sentence (example: the 2019 Nursing Entrance Exam).
- State the fixed time constraint (example: 6 hours total to study and complete).
- State the baseline: zero background beyond basic lived experience.
- On screen: the exam title card, the timer, and a one-line baseline caption.
- Done when a new viewer can repeat the challenge back in one sentence.

## B2. Stakes and incentives (0:45 to 1:15)

- State the pass line as an exact number (example: 70 out of 100).
- **Pass:** an immediate real-world reward, for example a local coffee shop visit and cookie b-roll.
- **Fail:** an immediate punitive task, for example eating an obscure insect or novelty item.
- Show the prop for the punishment now, so the viewer wants to see it used.
- Done when the viewer cares about the outcome and has a reason to stay for the score.

## B3. Test and immediate result (1:15 to 2:00)

- Cut quickly from the stakes to the test. Do not replay the study window; compress it to a short
  montage with the timer visible.
- Show the real test capture for a few seconds so the stakes feel earned.
- Score declaration on screen exactly as the scorer produced it (example: "89 out of 100").
- High-energy reaction beat, then straight into the reward or punishment payout b-roll.
- Done when the result has landed and the payoff has played. Nothing from B4 starts before this.

## B4. Technical synthesis (2:00 to 8:00)

This is the longest phase and the biggest drop-off risk. It is 6 minutes, split into three sections of
about 2 minutes each. Lead with the best material.

| Section | Content | Why it is in this order |
|---|---|---|
| 4a. Counterintuitive | Concepts that surprised the host or contradict common assumptions | Re-hooks the viewer right after the payoff |
| 4b. Patterns | Structural patterns that overlap across domains | Gives the series its transferable value |
| 4c. Core knowledge | The operational knowledge a candidate actually needs to pass | The part a returning or searching viewer wants |

Pacing rules for B4:

- One idea per beat, about 30 to 45 seconds each. If it needs longer, it is two ideas.
- A visual change at every beat. Slides and diagrams are built as Remotion shots, not static decks.
- Keep a visible running tally of "what the exam actually tested" so the section has a spine.
- Tie each concept back to a specific exam question when one exists. It makes the debrief concrete and
  proves the exam was real.
- Add the first-timer disclaimer from contract rule 6 where the domain calls for it.

Done when each of the three categories has been covered and nothing runs past its time box.

## B5. Sponsor and outro (8:00 to end)

- Transition into the sponsor naturally from the last concept (example: a platform for interactive
  STEM learning such as Brilliant). The sponsor is an example, not a commitment.
- Disclose the sponsorship out loud and in the upload settings.
- Plant the next-episode seed (example: the land surveyor entrance exam) with one concrete detail and
  one reason it is going to be hard.
- Done when the viewer knows what the next domain is and has a reason to subscribe.

## Variation budget

The arc is fixed. What may change per episode: the domain, the time constraint, the pass line, the
reward and punishment, the sponsor, and the visual treatment of B4. What may not change: the order of
the phases, the viewer contract, and the rule that the result is shown before the debrief starts.
